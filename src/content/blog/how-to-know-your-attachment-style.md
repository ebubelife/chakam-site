Most advice on how to know your attachment style starts with a quiz, and a quiz asks how you'd behave. That's the weak spot. Someone who spirals when a text goes unanswered can honestly tell a quiz, "I'm pretty relaxed about communication." Their reply-checking history would disagree.

So skip the self-portrait. Look at what you actually do in five ordinary situations. Your answers to those five will tell you more than a label ever will.

## What Your Attachment Style Actually Is

Attachment theory began with the British psychoanalyst John Bowlby, who argued that children bond with their caregivers as a matter of survival. In the 1970s, Mary Ainsworth watched how young children reacted when a parent left a room and came back, and sorted the reactions into patterns. In 1987, Cindy Hazan and Phillip Shaver [proposed in a widely cited paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3572722/) that adult romantic relationships run on the same system.

Researchers describe the patterns in a few different ways. The version most people use online has four styles:

- **Secure:** comfortable with closeness and comfortable with space. Distress passes without much drama.
- **Anxious** (sometimes called preoccupied): wants closeness and reassurance, and watches closely for signs of distance.
- **Avoidant** (sometimes called dismissive): values independence and gets uncomfortable when closeness turns intense.
- **Disorganized** (often called fearful-avoidant): wants closeness and fears it at the same time.

Two things to hold onto before the clues. This is a pattern, not a diagnosis. And almost nobody is a pure type.

## How to Know Your Attachment Style: 5 Clues From What You Actually Do

To know your attachment style, look at your behavior in five moments: when someone goes quiet, after a good date, during conflict, when someone says "I love you," and when someone gets close too fast. The reaction you repeat under stress, not the one you'd choose on a calm day, points to your style.

Answer as the version of you that shows up at 11pm on a Tuesday, not the version you'd like to be. If a clue fits two styles, that's normal.

### Clue 1: What You Do When They Go Quiet

They said they'd text tonight. It's 11:40 and there's nothing.

- **Anxious:** you spiral and assume the worst.
- **Avoidant:** you feel relieved, almost lighter.
- **Disorganized:** you panic, then push them away first.
- **Secure:** you assume they're busy and carry on with your evening.

Relief is the surprising one. People expect avoidance to look cold, but it often looks like a quiet exhale when the pressure to respond disappears. ### Clue 2: What You Do After a Good Date

The date went well. Nothing went wrong. Now what does your head do?

- **Anxious:** you replay every detail and wait for the next text.
- **Avoidant:** you're already thinking about your next few days alone.
- **Disorganized:** you feel excited, then suddenly anxious for no clear reason.
- **Secure:** you feel good and don't overthink it.

The tell here is what your mind does with a good outcome. Secure people let it be good. Everyone else finds something to do with the feeling.

### Clue 3: How You Handle Conflict

You've had a disagreement. It isn't a huge one, but it's unresolved.

- **Anxious:** you need to resolve it immediately and can't let it sit.
- **Avoidant:** you shut down and need to leave the room.
- **Disorganized:** you want to fix it and run from it at the same time.
- **Secure:** you stay calm, say what you feel, and move on.

Notice that the anxious and avoidant answers are opposite moves. One person needs to talk now, the other needs space now, and both feel the other is the problem. ### Clue 4: What "I Love You" Does to You

You'd expect this one to be pure joy. It often isn't.

- **Anxious:** it makes you want to hear it again immediately.
- **Avoidant:** it makes you feel a little exposed, even if you feel it too.
- **Disorganized:** it makes you happy, then suspicious of the happiness.
- **Secure:** it makes you feel closer, and that's the whole reaction.

### Clue 5: What You Do When Someone Gets Too Close Too Fast

Someone is texting all day, planning weekends, and talking about the future in week two.

- **Anxious:** you lean in harder, hoping it means something.
- **Avoidant:** you create distance without even meaning to.
- **Disorganized:** you get excited, then sabotage it.
- **Secure:** you set a boundary and see how they respond.

One caution. Speed like that can also be a manipulation tactic, which is a separate question covered in [how to tell if you're being love bombed](/blog/how-to-tell-if-youre-being-love-bombed/). This clue is about your reaction, whatever their motive.

## How to Know Your Attachment Style From Your Answers

Count which style showed up most. If four or five answers point the same way, you've probably found your default.

Two styles tied is common. Disorganized often looks exactly like that: a mix of anxious and avoidant answers, sometimes within a single situation.

Also check whether your answers hold across different people. If you're calm with friends and frantic with one partner, that pattern says as much about the dynamic as it does about you.

If you want a deeper read on the most common result, start with [the signs of an anxious attachment style](/blog/signs-of-anxious-attachment-style/).

None of this is a diagnosis. If these patterns are costing you sleep or relationships, a therapist who works with attachment will help you more than any article can.

## Your Attachment Style Isn't Fixed

An attachment style is a pattern your nervous system learned, and learned patterns can be relearned. Seeing the pattern clearly is the first step, and you've just done that.

After that, two things tend to help most: a consistent partner, and practicing the opposite move in low-stakes moments. Therapy speeds it up when the pattern runs deep. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you have more than one attachment style?

Yes, in practice. Many people lean mostly one way but show a different style with a particular partner or under heavy stress. That's why a single strong reaction proves little, and a pattern across several situations proves a lot.

### Can your attachment style change?

Yes. It tends to shift through consistent, safe experiences that repeatedly show closeness won't vanish the moment there's distance. That can come from a stable relationship, from therapy, or from deliberately practicing new responses. It rarely changes just by waiting.

### How accurate is an online attachment style quiz?

A quiz is a reasonable starting point, but it relies on you describing yourself, and self-description is where people slip. Treat the result as a hypothesis, then test it against how you actually behave in the five situations above.

### What's the difference between disorganized and fearful-avoidant attachment?

In everyday use, they're the same pattern under two names: wanting closeness and fearing it at once. Researchers sometimes draw finer lines between the terms, but for understanding your own behavior, you can treat them as one.

### How do I know if my partner is avoidant or just busy?

Look at the pattern over time, not one quiet week. A busy person's distance has a reason and an end date. Avoidant distance tends to appear when things get closer, then ease when the pressure drops.
