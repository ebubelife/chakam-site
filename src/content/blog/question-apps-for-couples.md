Most couples do not stop talking. They stop talking about anything that is not logistics. Bins, bills, what time you are leaving, who is picking up the thing. Months pass and you realise you know your partner's schedule better than you know what they are worried about.

That gap is the entire business model of question apps for couples. They hand you one prompt a day, you both answer, and you see each other's replies. The prompt is not the product. The five minutes of talking afterwards is.

Below are five worth knowing, compared using each app's own App Store listing on the US store on 26 September 2026. Every rating and date traces back there. Chakam is our app and it appears at the end, clearly marked, doing a different job from the rest.

## Why Use a Question App for Couples at All?

The honest answer is friction. You do not need an app to ask your partner a good question. You also will not, because there is never an obvious moment, and starting a real conversation from nothing feels heavier than it should.

An app solves three specific problems, and it is worth being precise about them.

- **It picks the time.** A notification at 8pm is a decision neither of you has to make.
- **It picks the question.** Most people are bad at generating good questions on demand, especially when tired.
- **It removes the subtext.** "The app asked this" is very different from "I need to ask you something." The second one makes people brace. The first one does not.

That third point is the one people underrate. A prompt gives you cover to say something real without it becoming A Conversation.

What it does not do is generate interest that is not there. If neither of you opens it after week two, the app was never the problem.

## The 5 Best Question Apps for Couples

All five are free to download and charge for the full library. Apple does not expose subscription prices in a verifiable way, so check the price screen in the app rather than trusting a figure in any article, this one included.

### Paired

[Paired](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/paired-couples-relationship/id1469609343) is the category leader by a distance, built around a daily question you both answer, where your partner's response unlocks only after you have written your own.

- **Rating:** 4.7 from 207,977 ratings, roughly four times the next app on this list.
- **Released:** September 2020, last updated September 2026.
- **Age rating:** 17+.
- **Watch for:** the listing says "scientifically proven to increase relationship satisfaction". There is research behind the approach in general, but a phrase that strong in marketing copy is worth treating as a claim rather than a finding.

### Evergreen

[Evergreen](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/evergreen-relationship-growth/id1573360122) pairs daily questions with quizzes and check-ins, so it leans more towards tracking how the relationship is doing over time.

- **Rating:** 4.8 from 54,509 ratings.
- **Released:** July 2021, last updated September 2026.
- **Age rating:** 17+.
- **Watch for:** the check-in structure suits couples who like measuring things. If one of you finds that clinical, it will be the reason you stop.

### Agapé

[Agapé](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/agap%C3%A9-feel-close-when-apart/id1507907556) sends one question a day, and unusually it is not only for partners. You can be matched with a friend or a parent, and the questions adapt to that relationship.

- **Rating:** 4.8 from 26,230 ratings.
- **Released:** October 2020, last updated April 2026.
- **Age rating:** 17+.
- **Watch for:** the listing states spicier questions are only sent when you are matched with a partner, which matters if you are also using it with family. Its name, "Feel Close When Apart", tells you where it is aimed.

### Gottman Card Decks

[Gottman Card Decks](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gottman-card-decks/id1292398843) comes from The Gottman Institute and is drawn from the card decks used in their couples workshop. It is the only app here whose questions come with decades of published relationship research behind them.

- **Rating:** 4.8 from 1,663 ratings, a small number that reflects a niche audience rather than a weak app.
- **Released:** October 2017, last updated June 2026.
- **The listing describes:** 22 card decks and more than 1,000 cards.
- **Watch for:** it is a deck of prompts, not a daily habit loop. Nothing nudges you, nothing tracks you, and nobody unlocks anything. If you want structure, this is the wrong shape. If you want quality questions for free, it is the best value here by a wide margin.

### Coral

[Coral](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/coral-couples-relationship/id1448861466) is aimed at intimacy specifically, mixing daily prompts with guided exercises for couples whose sex life has gone quiet.

- **Rating:** 4.6 from 3,510 ratings.
- **Released:** October 2019, last updated March 2026.
- **Age rating:** 17+.
- **Watch for:** this is the one on the list most likely to surface something neither of you was ready to discuss. That is the point of it, and also the reason to read the next section before you start.

## Are the Questions Actually Safe to Use?

Mostly, with one real exception that nobody warns you about.

Harmless questions are the default. "What is a small thing I do that you appreciate?" costs nothing. The risk sits in the deeper decks, which are exactly the ones people jump to when something already feels off.

A question is unsafe when it does one of these:

- **Asks you to rank or compare.** Anything involving exes, or "what would you change about me", has almost no upside and a large downside.
- **Surfaces a real incompatibility on a Tuesday night.** Children, money, monogamy and where you will live are worth discussing. They are not worth discovering at 11pm because an app shuffled them to the top.
- **Arrives during an unrelated argument.** Prompts do not know you are mid-conflict, and an intimacy question landing then reads as sarcasm.

Three habits make the whole thing safe:

1. **Either of you can skip, no reason required.** Agree this out loud before you start. A skip is not an answer.
2. **Do not do the deep decks first.** Build the habit on easy prompts for a couple of weeks.
3. **Treat a hard answer as information, not as an attack.** If you asked, you wanted the truth.

One more, and it is the important one. If your relationship involves fear, coercion or control, these apps are the wrong tool, and a prompt asking you to be vulnerable can put you in a worse position. That situation needs a qualified person, not an app.

## Can Question Apps for Couples Help a Long-Distance Relationship?

This is where they earn their keep, more than for couples who live together.

Distance does not usually kill a relationship through conflict. It kills it through thinning. Your days stop overlapping, the small stuff has nowhere to go, and calls become status reports: work was fine, I am tired, how was yours. Nothing is wrong and nothing is happening.

A daily question inserts one non-logistical exchange into a day with no shared events in it. That is a small thing that compounds, and it fixes the specific failure mode of distance rather than a general lack of romance.

Two practical notes. Time zones matter more than the app's feature list, so pick one that lets you answer independently rather than requiring you both to be present. And the asynchronous unlock, where you must answer before seeing their reply, works better across distance than it does at home, because it guarantees both sides actually write something instead of one person carrying it.

Agapé is explicitly built for this. Paired works well because the unlock mechanic is central. Gottman Card Decks works if you are disciplined enough to run it yourselves over a call.

## Can Questions Fix a Shaky Relationship?

No. Being straight about this is more useful than selling you hope.

A daily prompt cannot repair a betrayal, resolve a genuine incompatibility, or make someone want to be there. If one person has already checked out, a question app becomes one more thing they do not open, and you will read that silence as further evidence, which it is.

What questions genuinely help with is a narrower and more common problem: two people who still want this but have drifted into logistics and lost the habit of curiosity. That is not a shaky relationship, it is a neglected one, and prompts work well on neglect.

There is one honest exception. A question app can be useful as a diagnostic. Two weeks of prompts will tell you very quickly whether the other person is willing to engage at all. That answer is worth having, even when it is not the one you wanted.

If things are genuinely shaky, the order matters: a conversation about the actual problem first, professional help if it is serious, and prompts later as maintenance once there is something to maintain.

## What Makes a Good Couple Question?

Once you see the pattern, you can tell within a second whether a prompt is worth answering.

- **It cannot be answered with one word.** "Do you love me" is not a question, it is a request for reassurance.
- **It asks about specifics, not categories.** "What was the best part of your week" beats "are you happy".
- **It is answerable by both of you.** A question only one person has material for turns into an interview.
- **It has no correct answer.** If one response would clearly disappoint, you are testing, not asking.
- **It points forward at least sometimes.** Nostalgia is pleasant. What you both want next is where the useful conversations are.

## What If Only One of You Is Into It?

Extremely common, and worth handling directly rather than escalating.

The usual failure is that the enthusiastic partner treats engagement with the app as a measure of love. It is not. Plenty of people find scheduled emotional prompts genuinely uncomfortable, and that is temperament rather than indifference. Pushing turns a pleasant habit into a loyalty test, and the app becomes a thing you argue about.

What tends to work: propose a short trial with an end date, one week, and let them opt out cleanly at the end. Pick the least earnest app you can find. Let them answer in one line without commentary. And notice whether they engage with the conversation afterwards even when they will not use the app, because that is the part that actually matters.

If they will not do either, the app has told you something, and the conversation you need is not a prompt.

## How Often Should You Actually Use One?

Daily is what every app defaults to, and it is more than most couples sustain.

The pattern that lasts tends to be two or three times a week, deliberately, rather than daily for eleven days followed by nothing. Miss a few days and skip them rather than answering a backlog, because catching up turns it into homework.

The realistic lifespan of one of these apps is a few months. That is not failure. The habit of asking each other something real is the thing you keep, and at some point you stop needing the notification to do it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do question apps for couples actually work?

They work at the thing they do, which is causing a conversation to happen that otherwise would not. Whether that improves the relationship depends on what you do with the five minutes afterwards. The apps that fail are almost always the ones nobody opens after a fortnight, rather than the ones with bad questions.

### Do both partners need to install the app?

For most of them, yes. Paired, Evergreen, Agapé and Coral are built around two linked accounts and an unlock mechanic. Gottman Card Decks is the exception and works fine from one phone, which makes it the easiest to try when your partner is not sold yet.

### Are these apps good for new relationships?

Some, with care. Early on, a deep prompt can feel like an interrogation before there is enough trust to hold it. Stick to light decks for the first months. If you are still in the [talking stage and overthinking every message](/blog/overthinking-texts-talking-stage/), a couples app is probably premature.

### Is it weird to need an app to talk to your partner?

No. What it actually replaces is the conversation you would have had if you were not tired, and both of you are tired. Nobody thinks a recipe means you cannot cook.

### What should we do if a question starts an argument?

Stop answering the prompt and deal with what came up, or agree out loud to come back to it when you are both rested. The mistake is finishing the exercise while the real thing sits unaddressed. If the same subject detonates every time, that topic needs a proper conversation, and possibly a professional, rather than a better-worded question.
