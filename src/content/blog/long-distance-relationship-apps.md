Distance does not usually end a relationship through a fight. It ends it through thinning. Your days stop overlapping, calls turn into status reports, and one evening you realise you have nothing to say that is not admin.

The long distance relationship apps below are worth knowing because each one attacks a specific version of that problem. Most roundups list ten couple apps that all do daily questions. These five do genuinely different jobs, which is the only reason to install more than one.

Every figure comes from each app's own App Store listing, checked on the US store on 27 September 2026. Chakam is our app and appears at the end, clearly marked, doing something none of the others do.

## What Makes a Long Distance Relationship App Worth Installing?

Distance breaks four things, and an app is only useful if it fixes one of them.

- **Overlap.** You are awake at different times, so live conversation keeps failing.
- **Presence.** You stop being a background part of each other's day and become an appointment.
- **Depth.** You both default to logistics because real conversation needs a moment neither of you can schedule.
- **Shared experience.** Couples who live together accumulate small joint memories. At distance you have to manufacture them.

Long distance relationship apps that do not address one of those are widgets you will delete in a fortnight. Countdown timers are pleasant and fix nothing.

## The 5 Best Long Distance Relationship Apps

All are free to download, most charge for the better parts, and Apple does not publish subscription prices in a checkable way. Read the price screen in the app rather than any figure in an article.

### Marco Polo

[Marco Polo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/marco-polo-video-messenger/id912561374) is video you record and your partner watches whenever they wake up. It is the single best answer to a bad time-zone gap, and it is not a couples app at all, which is part of why it works.

- **Rating:** 4.8 from 2,160,854 ratings, by far the largest sample on this list.
- **Released:** October 2014, last updated September 2026.
- **Age rating:** 12+.
- **Why it matters here:** a voice note carries tone. A video carries a face, a room, a mood. Across eight time zones that is much closer to company than a text thread.
- **Watch for:** the listing leans hard on privacy and no ads. Both are good, but the real limit is effort. Recording video daily is a bigger ask than typing, and couples who drift usually drift here first.

### Locket Widget

[Locket](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/locket-widget/id1600525061) puts a photo from your partner straight onto your home screen, replaced whenever they send a new one.

- **Rating:** 4.8 from 571,217 ratings.
- **Released:** December 2021, last updated September 2026.
- **Age rating:** 12+.
- **Why it matters here:** this is the presence problem, solved almost perfectly. You are not opening an app to check on them. They are simply there, several times a day, with no effort from you at all.
- **Watch for:** it is built for friends rather than couples, so there is no couple-specific structure. It also creates a small obligation, because a widget that has shown the same photo for four days is its own quiet message.

### Agapé

[Agapé](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/agap%C3%A9-feel-close-when-apart/id1507907556) sends one question a day that you both answer, and it is named for exactly this use case.

- **Rating:** 4.8 from 26,229 ratings.
- **Released:** October 2020, last updated April 2026.
- **Age rating:** 17+.
- **Why it matters here:** it fixes depth. One non-logistical exchange in a day that contains no shared events is small and compounds.
- **Watch for:** the listing says spicier questions only arrive when you are matched with a partner rather than a friend or parent, which matters because the app is built for several relationships at once. We covered the wider category in [question apps for couples](/blog/question-apps-for-couples/).

### Hearo

[Hearo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hearo-watch-together/id1385627895) synchronises streaming across two devices with voice chat on top, so you can watch the same thing at the same time.

- **Rating:** 4.3 from 7,731 ratings, the lowest here and worth taking seriously.
- **Released:** July 2018, last updated February 2026.
- **Age rating:** 12+.
- **Why it matters here:** shared experience. Watching a bad film together and talking through it is the closest thing to an ordinary evening that distance allows.
- **Watch for:** watch-party apps depend on streaming services that change their players without warning, and that is generally what sits behind a rating like this one. Expect it to break occasionally. You both also still need your own subscriptions.

### Between

[Between](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/between-couples-love-tracker/id458035189) is a private messenger and shared archive for exactly two people, with photos, notes and a timeline in one place.

- **Rating:** 4.8 from 21,192 ratings.
- **Released:** November 2011, making it the oldest app here by some distance.
- **Age rating:** 4+, the only one on this list rated for all ages.
- **Why it matters here:** it builds the joint memory that living together produces for free. Two years in, the archive is the thing you actually value.
- **Watch for:** a second messenger is a real cost. Couples who keep it usually treat it as the memory box and keep chatting where they already chat.

## Do Long Distance Relationship Apps Actually Help?

They help with the mechanics, which is not nothing, and they do nothing about the reasons.

An app can make it easier to talk across a nine-hour gap. It cannot manufacture the wish to. If one of you has quietly decided this is temporary, the widget becomes another thing that goes unanswered, and you will read that silence accurately.

The honest version is that these apps lower the effort required to stay close. Lower effort matters a lot when both people are trying and tired. It matters not at all when one person has stopped.

## How Do You Handle Time Zones Without Resenting Each Other?

Time zones cause more long-distance breakups than jealousy does, and the resentment is rarely about the hours themselves. It is about who keeps paying for them.

Three things help.

- **Stop treating live calls as the default.** Asynchronous video and voice notes carry most of what a call carries without either of you staying up until two. That is why Marco Polo appears first on this list.
- **Alternate the sacrifice explicitly.** If the same person is always up late, that imbalance accumulates whether or not anyone mentions it. Say out loud whose turn it is.
- **Protect one real overlap a week, not daily contact.** A proper unrushed hour beats seven tired fifteen-minute calls, and it is much easier to keep.

## How Often Should You Actually Talk?

Less than most long-distance couples think, and more deliberately.

Daily contact is the usual default, and it tends to produce daily logistics: what you ate, how work was, when you are free. It feels like closeness and is mostly reporting. Meanwhile the pressure to speak every day turns a missed evening into a small crisis.

What tends to work better is a rhythm both of you actually agreed to rather than one that formed by accident, plus permission to miss a day without it meaning anything. If a skipped call reliably produces a conversation about the skipped call, the frequency is too high for your real lives.

Watch the shape rather than the volume. If you are noticing that [one of you is always the one reaching out](/blog/one-sided-texting-how-to-know/), that gap says more than the total message count does.

## Is Location Sharing a Trust Thing or a Control Thing?

It can be either, and the difference is not subtle once you look for it.

Location sharing is fine when it is mutual, mundane and boring: you both have it on, nobody checks, and it exists so someone knows you landed. It has gone wrong when it is one-directional, when it gets checked, or when being unreachable for two hours requires an explanation.

The test is simple. If one of you turned it off tomorrow, would that be a shrug or an incident? If it would be an incident, the app is not creating trust, it is standing in for the absence of it, and no feature fixes that.

The same applies to read receipts, online indicators and anything else that lets you monitor a person from another continent. Distance already makes small silences feel enormous. Tools that let you inspect those silences usually make it worse, not better.

## Can an App Keep a Long Distance Relationship Alive?

No, and the apps that claim otherwise are selling you a streak.

Long distance relationship apps cannot supply motivation. What actually keeps long distance working is boring and well established: a real end date, genuine shared plans, and both people having a full life where they are. Couples who make it are usually the ones who know roughly when the distance stops. Couples who struggle are often the ones who never set that, because without it every hard week is evidence about the whole future rather than about the week.

Where apps genuinely contribute is friction. They make the good habits cheaper to keep. That is a real contribution, and it is a smaller one than the marketing implies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best app for long distance relationships?

There is no single best one, because they solve different failures. Marco Polo if your problem is time zones, Locket if you have stopped feeling present in each other's days, Agapé if you have drifted into logistics, Hearo if you miss doing things together, Between if you want a shared archive. Start with the one that matches your actual complaint.

### Do we both need to install the same apps?

Yes for all five. Every one of them is built on two linked accounts, and an app only one of you opens is worse than no app, because it turns into a scoreboard of who is trying.

### How many of these should we actually use?

One or two. The common mistake is installing four in a burst of enthusiasm and abandoning all of them within a month. Pick the single problem that bothers you most right now and solve only that.

### Are long distance relationship apps safe to use?

The apps themselves are mainstream and mostly fine. The risk is in what you switch on inside them. Photos, shared location and message archives are all things you are trusting a company and a person with, so check what a privacy label says is collected, and be deliberate about location in particular.

### How long can a long distance relationship realistically last?

The published research points less at a duration than at whether there is a plan. Couples with a specific, agreed end to the distance tend to do well over surprisingly long stretches. Couples with no end date tend to struggle regardless of how well they communicate, because every difficult week starts reading as evidence about the whole future.
